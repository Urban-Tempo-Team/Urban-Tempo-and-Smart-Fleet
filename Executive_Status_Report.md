## Top Risks

| Risk | Business Impact | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Tight launch timeline | A delayed pilot pushes back savings | Launch as a one-depot pilot with core features only |
| Limited historical vehicle data | Early predictions may be less accurate | Start with rule-based alerts and refine with pilot data |
| Staff adoption | Benefits depend on depot teams using the system | Training owner assigned, with a champion at each depot |

## Key Decision

The **passenger-crowding feature** requested by the Transit Director was **deferred to Phase 2** by the Change Control Board. This protects the launch date and budget without losing the idea.

## Strategic Next Steps

1. **Launch the pilot on November 20** with predictive vehicle alerts, automated work orders and live route updates.
2. **Measure pilot results:** breakdowns avoided, repair costs and fleet availability.
3. **Bring a Phase 2 proposal** for fleet-wide rollout and the crowding feature for sponsor approval, funded partly by the projected budget underrun.

## Business Value

The pilot moves Urban Tempo from **reactive, spreadsheet-based maintenance to predictive maintenance**. This is expected to reduce unexpected breakdowns and emergency repair costs, keep more vehicles in service, and improve reliability for riders.
